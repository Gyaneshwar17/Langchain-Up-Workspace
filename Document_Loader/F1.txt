Formula 1, commonly known as F1, represents the absolute pinnacle of global motorsport and high-performance automotive engineering. Sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), it features open-wheel, single-seater race cars competing on iconic purpose-built tracks and historic street circuits around the world. Each annual season consists of a series of international races known as Grands Prix, where teams and drivers battle for championship glory. Drivers compete for the prestigious Drivers' Championship based on individual points accumulated throughout the season, while teams compete for the Constructors' Championship by combining the points of both their drivers.

The sport demands extraordinary physical fitness, lighting-fast reaction times, and immense mental endurance from its drivers as they withstand extreme G-forces. The cars themselves are marvels of modern technology, powered by highly efficient 1.6-liter turbocharged V6 hybrid power units paired with advanced energy recovery systems. Reaching breathtaking speeds in excess of 350 km/h (217 mph), these machines rely on intricate aerodynamic design—featuring complex wings, undertrays, and ground-effect tunnels—to generate massive downforce for cornering at incredible speeds.
